      ABSTRACT Continual network intrusion detection must adapt to evolving traffic while preserving benign
      and previously learned attack behavior. Existing approaches commonly respond to drift by replaying a large
      buffer or updating the whole predictor, which couples unrelated feature groups, increases adaptation cost,
      and provides limited evidence about what changed. This paper presents Drift-Attributed Subspace Plasticity
      (DASP), a modular continual-learning framework that localizes distribution shift to feature-group subspaces,
      updates only the routed representation modules, and retains a shared trainable classifier. DASP combines layer-
      and module-wise discrepancy monitoring, a top-k attribution router, strict structural freezing, class-balanced
      bounded rehearsal, new-sample weighting, and a performance-gated emergency repair path. The method is
      evaluated under a prediction-first, one-window delayed-label protocol on five network-flow datasets, ten
      drift conditions, five stream seeds, two model seeds, and eight methods. The confirmation matrix contains
      4,000 runs and compares DASP with seven locked alternatives. Across all eight methods, DASP obtains the
      highest observed mean Macro-F1 (0.9269), followed by SSF (0.9191) and Experience Replay (0.9036). SSF
      is the prespecified primary comparator: DASP wins 383 of 500 paired cells with a mean gap of +0.0078,
      and the locked-matrix sign test gives p = 2.56 × 10−34 . DASP reduces adaptation time by a factor of 1.90
      and gradient evaluations by a factor of 1.56 relative to SSF. The method performs no logged full-model or
      all-representation-module update; the shared classifier remains trainable, and all 16,000 observed repair
      records activate at most three of four representation modules. The main systems trade-off is lower forward
      throughput caused by the parallel modular backbone. These results support the integrated localized-repair
      system as an effective and auditable alternative to indiscriminate online retraining under the implemented
      supervision paths; they do not isolate the predictive contribution of the attribution router or establish a strict
      shared total-label-exposure comparison.
